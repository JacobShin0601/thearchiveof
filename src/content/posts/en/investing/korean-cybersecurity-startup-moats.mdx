The temptation in cybersecurity is to translate a good technical explanation directly into business quality. Security matters, but a project does not automatically become recurring, high-margin revenue. Conversely, a small product can become durable when it is embedded in a customer’s data, workflow, and distribution channel.

This is a public-evidence test of two observations. Monoly may be more than another SaaS product if it becomes a control layer between enterprise data stores and the SaaS or AI services employees use. 78ResearchLab may be more than a security-services shop if its research and field learning accumulate as PurpleHound product IP. Non-public financing discussions, customer terms, and valuations are not evidence in this article.

## The questions that matter

| Question | Supporting observation | Falsifier |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Does Monoly’s control layer reuse? | One policy, storage, and audit architecture can serve more use cases | Every customer requires new connectors and proportional delivery effort |
| Does 78ResearchLab’s research become product IP? | Scenarios become automated and renewals rise | Core researchers still recreate the service for every deployment |
| Is there a global value-realization path? | Recurring revenue, gross margin, channel leverage, and international repeatability | Overseas selling, certification, and support consume the software margin |

## Monoly: replacing SaaS, or controlling the data around it?

Monoly says it can store and manage SaaS data in a secure vault in the cloud environment selected by the customer, apply customer-owned access policies, and maintain an audit trail for sharing. It describes Monoly Enclave as a secure SaaS gateway. [Monoly](https://monoly.com/index_en.html)

```text
Customer-selected storage and policy
              ↓
Monoly Enclave: control, audit, sharing trail
              ↓
Existing SaaS and external AI use
```

That diagram is a compressed reading of the published product description, not a claim that every SaaS or AI integration has been implemented. The investment question is simpler: how much of an integration, policy, and operating procedure can be reused for the next customer?

Public reporting says Monoly raised a Pre-A round in 2026 with new and returning investors, and cites work in a Korean zero-trust pilot and financial-sector SaaS security environments. That is meaningful evidence of a demanding reference environment, not evidence of recurring revenue or a disclosed valuation. [Edaily Marketin](https://marketin.edaily.co.kr/News/Read?newsId=04247606645320016)

Monoly’s approximately KRW 18 billion seed financing in 2022 is public. It is financing amount, not a historical or current enterprise value. [Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20221014_0002048499)

The upside case is a reusable control plane: policy, audit, and data-location decisions remain useful as a customer expands SaaS and AI use. The bear case is custom integration: revenue grows with engineers and maintenance workload. Both can create customer value; only one has software-like operating leverage.

## 78ResearchLab: does research compound into a product curve?

78ResearchLab presents PurpleHound as a BAS platform that simulates attacker techniques to validate an organization’s defensive posture. In a company-hosted 2026 interview, management said 2025 revenue grew more than 50%, PurpleHound launched at the end of 2024, and the product won its first financial-sector deployment in 2025. Those are company statements—not audited ARR, retention, or independently validated performance data. [78ResearchLab interview](https://78researchlab.com/news/?bmode=view&idx=170005464&q=YToxOntzOjEyOiJrZXl3b3JkX3R5cGUiO3M6MzoiYWxsIjt9&t=board)

```text
Offensive research → scenarios → PurpleHound automation
        ↑                                  ↓
Field learning ← customer validation and detection gaps
```

Consulting is not necessarily the enemy of productization. It can be the learning loop: researchers find a path, encode it into a maintained scenario and report, then reduce onboarding work for the next customer. But that narrative becomes an economic moat only when product mix, gross margin, and retention rise together.

One public distribution signal is clearer. eNsecure announced a December 2025 partnership covering PurpleHound’s technology, sales, and marketing. It supports a channel hypothesis; it does not establish contract value, exclusivity, or sourced revenue. [eNsecure](https://ensecure.tistory.com/133?category=842444)

## A growing Korean market is not the same thing as a global exit

The Korean government and KISIA survey puts 2024 domestic information-security revenue at roughly KRW 7.12 trillion, up 15.9% year over year. Information-security exports, however, were about KRW 124.2 billion, down 15.9%. That does not prove demand is weak, nor does it prove Korean cybersecurity is automatically undervalued. Domestic demand, product mix, international distribution, support cost, and exit liquidity are separate variables. [2025 Information Security Industry Survey](https://eiec.kdi.re.kr/policy/materialView.do?num=273123)

Strategic acquirers tend to buy a hard-to-build product capability, an installed workflow, or a channel that accelerates distribution. A global cybersecurity multiple is therefore an outcome of comparable ARR quality, growth, margin, and customer durability—not a starting assumption for an early private company.

## Current view

| Hypothesis | Current assessment | What would change the assessment |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Monoly’s software moat | Conditionally supported | Integration hours, expansion within existing accounts, maintenance cost |
| 78ResearchLab’s service-to-product transition | Conditionally supported | Product ARR mix, renewal and expansion, automated scenario coverage, deployment cost |
| Global value realization | Insufficient evidence | Overseas revenue, channel-sourced sales, support cost, credible strategic fit |

The interesting feature of these companies is not simply that security is important. One may become a common enterprise data-control layer; the other may turn offensive research into repeatable product IP. An investment conclusion should wait for recurring revenue, gross profit, customer expansion, and diluted ownership to confirm that potential.
