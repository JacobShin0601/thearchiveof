AI data centers are scarce. In many markets, power—not GPUs—is the binding constraint, and customers are leasing capacity years before they need it. <strong>Applied Digital Corporation (NASDAQ: APLD)</strong> has disclosed 1.41GW of contracted IT capacity and \$35.731B of minimum lease payments.

That is an impressive commercial result. It is not, by itself, an equity valuation.

$$ Contracted\ MW \rightarrow Operational\ MW \rightarrow Base\ Rent \rightarrow NOI \rightarrow Project\ Cash\ Flow \rightarrow Capital\ Providers \rightarrow Common\ Equity\ Value\ per\ Share $$

## 1. What APLD actually sells

HPC Hosting is closer to developing and leasing AI-ready real estate than to renting GPUs. APLD supplies dense power, buildings, liquid cooling, and site operations; tenants operate their own computing infrastructure. Base rent, tenant cost recoveries, and tenant build services therefore have different economics. ChronoScale and legacy hosting also require separate SOTP treatment rather than being folded into an HPC real-estate multiple.

## 2. Revenue quality: \$341.9M is not all recurring rent

FY2027 Q1 consolidated revenue was \$341.9M and HPC Hosting revenue was \$262.6M. But base rent was \$65.8M, tenant build services were \$183.5M, and tenant cost recoveries were \$13.3M. HPC NOI was \$58.8M.

The \$183.5M is build-service revenue, not the year-over-year increase of \$157.2M. It can be important, but it should not receive the same recurring-revenue multiple as a lease payment. The most useful starting point for recurring asset economics is base rent and its conversion as delivered MW grows.

## 3. Contracted capacity is not planned capacity

| Polaris Forge 1 | 400 | 400 |
| Polaris Forge 2 | 200 | 300 |
| Polaris Forge 3 | 300 | 300 |
| Delta Forge 1 | 300 | 300 |
| Delta Forge 2 | 210 | 210 |
| **Total** | **1,410** | **1,510** |

The 100MW difference matters: planned capacity is not contracted cash flow. Note 17 disclosed \$468.0M of minimum lease payments in FY2027, \$1,579.5M in FY2028, \$2,360.6M in FY2029, \$2,371.4M in FY2030 and FY2031, and \$26,580.2M thereafter—\$35.731B in total. These are minimum-payment commitments, not a complete annual cash-flow forecast.

## 4. Contracted MW must become operational MW

Polaris Forge 1 had 175MW in operation at August-end; a further 75MW was delivered on October 1, taking operating capacity to 250MW. A contract should be tracked through construction, ready-for-service, tenant delivery, and rent commencement. In a capital-intensive data center, delivery dates can matter more than contract announcements.

## 5. Power and time may be the moat

Grid-connected power, substations, liquid cooling, appropriate land, and construction speed are scarce together. That scarcity can create genuine customer value. It does not prove permanent excess returns. Investors should distinguish a power purchase agreement from deliverable power, ask who bears incremental generation and transmission risk, and test whether a campus remains competitive after the first tenant's contract expires.

## 6. An 89% NOI margin is not an ROIC

$$ NOI\ Margin=NOI/Base\ Rent,\qquad Yield\ on\ Cost=Stabilized\ NOI/Total\ Development\ Cost $$

The quarter's \$58.8M NOI on \$65.8M of base rent is a positive operating datapoint. NOI is still before corporate cash cost, maintenance CAPEX, cash taxes, financing cost, and development CAPEX. A consistent DCF must not subtract interest twice: discount FCFF and deduct debt once, or discount FCFE and do not deduct debt again.

## 7. Residual CAPEX and capital claims determine common value

Q1 CAPEX was about \$2.07B. Company materials cite approximately \$11M–\$13M per MW for selected campuses, but multiplying that range across 1.41GW does not create an official total project cost. The preliminary model used a proxy for residual construction cost, not disclosed project-level residual CAPEX.

At August-end, redeemable non-controlling interests were about \$2.01B. Series G preferred stock was about \$124.9M and subsequently converted into about 5.2M common shares. A model must remove the preferred claim when it adds the conversion shares, otherwise it double counts one claim. Debt, restricted cash, convertibles, warrants, Macquarie rights, and NCI must be handled in one equity bridge.

## 8. Preliminary valuation and reverse valuation

| Scenario | Discount rate | NOI margin | Cost/MW | Preliminary value/share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | 11.0% | 80% | \$13M | \$0.00 |
| Base | 8.5% | 86% | \$12M | \$5.02 |
| Bull | 7.0% | 89% | \$11M | \$26.18 |

These are provisional outputs, not price targets. The book-asset proxy, tenant-funded construction, capital claims, post-2031 rent allocation, re-letting cost, and vacancy risk all need further audit.

Still, the reverse-valuation message is useful: \$22.44 appears to require some combination of lower residual CAPEX, cheaper capital, higher re-letting value, additional positive-NPV MW, and less dilution. A good business and a good price are different propositions.

## Conclusion

APLD may own scarce and valuable AI-infrastructure capability. But owning AI infrastructure is not the same as owning its economic profits. The relevant question is not contracted power alone; it is the cash flow per share remaining after every construction and capital-provider claim is paid.
