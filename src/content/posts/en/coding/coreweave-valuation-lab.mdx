## Three illustrative cases

| Assumption | Bear | Base | Bull |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Backlog unlevered cash-flow margin | 10% | 20% | 30% |
| Net PV of other business | \$35B | \$60B | \$90B |
| Discount rate | 12% | 12% | 12% |
| Assumed shares | 559M | 559M | 559M |
| Estimated value per share | \$0.00 | \$54.64 | \$124.56 |

These are analyst assumptions, not company forecasts. **Bear's zero applies an equity floor to a negative modeled result; it is not a bankruptcy prediction.** The \$35B, \$60B and \$90B net values for renewals and new business are explicitly assumed, not independently observed. At 12%, the Base \$60B is equivalent to about \$10.6B per year in net cash flows for ten years; this is an illustration, not a forecast. Other-business PV must exclude cash flows already counted in backlog and include associated incremental investment and costs.

## Method and limits

Disclosed backlog recognition buckets are 36%, 38%, and 26%. We assume even recognition within each, and place the last bucket in years five through eight. At 12%, the revenue PV is about \$90.85B. Applying a 20% hypothetical unlevered cash margin and subtracting the \$4.23B discounted value of an assumed \$5B already collected yields backlog cash-flow PV of about \$13.95B.

Add the assumed \$60B of other business and subtract \$43.40B of net debt including finance leases. That yields \$30.54B of equity, divided by 559M proxy shares. The cash-flow measure excludes financing payments; operating lease costs remain in operating expenses, so operating leases are not separately subtracted as debt.

**559M shares is not current shares outstanding.** It is a proxy for Q3 weighted-average basic shares. Convertible and share compensation dilution, capital payables, customer-specific prepayments, receivables and remaining deployment costs are not fully identifiable. The \$5B prepayment input is a hypothetical allocation, not a verified balance. Modeling note conversion requires also removing the corresponding debt claim.

Changing the discount rate leaves the separate other-business PV input fixed, so this is not a fully integrated DCF sensitivity. Scenarios simplify correlated changes in renewal pricing, utilization and financing. Later price moves inform the analysis; they are not account returns or an automatic score of this tentative decision.
