A good business and a good entry price are different questions. Holding exit value constant, entry price and subsequent dilution can radically change investor returns.

## Calculation

2031 equity value is modeled as 2031 net income times exit P/E:

$$
Exit\ equity\ value = Revenue_{2031} \times Operating\ margin \times (1-Tax) \times Exit\ P/E
$$

Investor proceeds equal exit value times the ownership bought at entry, then ownership after subsequent dilution:

$$
Exit\ proceeds = Exit\ equity\ value \times \frac{Investment}{Pre-money + Investment} \times (1-Dilution)
$$

The maximum pre-money is solved backward to meet the target IRR. Actual terms may include liquidation preferences, participating preferred stock, redemption rights, drag-along provisions, and follow-on rights. This is a simplified common-equivalent model that sets those rights to zero.

## The Lab’s most important use

The purpose is not to admire a large Bull-case future value. It is to test whether a proposed entry price leaves the required return in the Base case. When credit loss, funding cost, and revenue recognition become clearer, the 2027 revenue and margin assumptions should be revised first.

Disclosure: This is a personal investment-research tool, not investment advice. Private investments carry substantial uncertainty around liquidity, dilution, preferred-stock rights, and exit timing.
