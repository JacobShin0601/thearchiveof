## What it calculates

Terminal revenue comes from customers and revenue per customer. Product/subscription and services revenue have separate gross-margin assumptions; platform fixed cost and recurring delivery/support cost are then deducted.

$$
Exit\ proceeds = \max(Exit\ EV - Net\ debt, 0) \times \frac{Investment}{Pre\text{-}money + Investment} \times (1-Dilution)
$$

The maximum pre-money is the reverse calculation for a target IRR. When terminal equity value is negative or too small, the Lab says the target is infeasible instead of concealing the result at zero.

## How to use it

1. Pick Bear, Base, or Bull. They are falsifiable assumption bundles, not forecasts.
2. Change product mix and recurring delivery cost. This is where a moat must turn into margin.
3. Change exit EV/revenue, dilution, and entry pre-money. A better company is not automatically a better-priced investment.
4. Read the sensitivity table to see what exit condition the productization story actually needs.

## What it excludes

The common-equivalent model excludes liquidation preferences, participation rights, option-pool timing, tax, transaction cost, cash flow, customer concentration, security incidents, and contract-specific terms. “Maximum pre-money” is a reverse-engineering prompt for deeper diligence, not a negotiated term sheet.
