Notes disappear more easily than we expect.

A sentence in a notes app, a useful conversation, the code behind an analysis, and the sources left open in a browser all end up in different places. At the time, the idea feels complete. A few months later, only the conclusion remains and the reasoning that produced it is gone.

I did not create The Archive to publish more often. I created it to make **past thinking reusable**.

## Preserve the decision process, not just the conclusion

Suppose I record only the sentence, “Interest rates are likely to fall.” Months later, I can check whether the forecast was correct, but little else. A real decision requires more context:

- Which inflation and employment data did I use?
- What was already reflected in market prices?
- Which assumptions made a rate cut appear more likely?
- How did that view lead to a decision about portfolio duration?
- What new evidence would make me change my mind?

Those questions make a wrong outcome useful. They help distinguish bad reasoning from a reasonable decision followed by bad luck.

That is why these articles try to separate **claims, evidence, assumptions, inference, and conditions for revision**. The purpose is less to display finished knowledge than to preserve the path by which an idea was formed.

## Why put different fields in one place?

Investing, AI & AX, mathematics, and hands-on projects appear to be separate subjects. In practice, the questions that interest me often share one underlying structure:

> How should we interpret uncertain information and make decisions in a constrained world?

In investing, a view must become a position. In AI, a model’s potential must become a working process. Mathematics provides the language of probability, estimation, and optimization. Projects in the Lab test whether those ideas survive contact with reality.

Keeping them in one archive makes it easier to preserve the connections that disappear when each field is treated as an isolated folder.

## Public documents put useful pressure on thinking

Private notes tolerate phrases such as “I know what I mean.” Public writing does not. A claim must be stated clearly, facts must be separated from interpretation, and numbers and sources must be checked again.

That pressure is uncomfortable but useful. Publishing is not a declaration that I am always right. It is a way to let someone else inspect **why I thought this was right**.

Articles may change when new evidence arrives. Important revisions should carry a date, and a changed conclusion should include an explanation. Durable research is not writing that has been frozen after publication; it is writing that can survive disagreement and revision.

## Written for people, readable by AI

The first reader of this archive is a person. A title should reveal the question, the argument should make sense from the text alone, and every cited source should be open to inspection.

Still, as more discovery happens through search and AI agents, it is strategically useful for good writing to be machine-readable as well. Stable URLs, explicit dates and topics, clear headings, and inspectable sources reduce the chance that a search engine or an agent will detach a claim from its context.

This does not mean the articles are written to please agents. The order is the other way around:

> A document that is clear and verifiable for people is usually easier for machines to read accurately.

Being “agent-friendly” is therefore not the protagonist of this archive. It is a **distribution and rediscovery advantage** produced by well-structured public documents.

## An archive, not a feed

Feeds demand something noticeable now. Archives demand something worth finding later.

For that reason, this site values the following standards more than publishing frequency:

- begin each article with one clear question whenever possible;
- distinguish facts, interpretation, and personal judgment;
- state the relevant date and the assumptions that matter;
- link sources, calculations, and code when possible;
- explain meaningful revisions instead of silently overwriting them; and
- keep the Korean original and English edition aligned to the same claims and evidence.

I will not meet these standards perfectly every time. But the purpose of this archive is to leave documents that can still be examined, challenged, and connected after the initial moment of publication has passed.
